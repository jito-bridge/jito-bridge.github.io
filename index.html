Jito Bridge.

A Jito Bridge carries assets such as SOL, USDC and wrapped ETH from another chain onto Solana, landing them in a wallet that can stake with Jito, trade on Solana DEXs or hold JitoSOL for liquid staking yield. You choose the source chain, the bridge quotes the amount that will arrive on Solana together with its fee, and you sign a transaction that keeps custody in your own wallet from start to finish. Because Solana settles in well under a second, the Solana side of the transfer confirms almost as soon as the source-chain transaction is final.

How the estimate is calculated

Fee = 0.25 units + 0.3% of the amount, rounded to two decimal places and capped at the amount sent. The remaining amount uses the same units, at a 1:1 conversion. Time starts at 2 minutes, adds 1 minute per 2,500 units or part thereof, and is capped at 12 minutes. The displayed range adds 2 minutes to that estimate.

What a Jito Bridge Does

A Jito Bridge locks or burns your asset on its home chain and credits the equivalent amount on Solana, so value reaches the Jito ecosystem while custody never leaves your own wallet. When you deposit, the bridge contract on the source chain receives your asset and a matching amount appears in your Solana wallet once the source transaction is final — often within a minute or two. The amount that will arrive, the fee and the estimated wait are shown before you sign, so there is no guesswork at the moment of approval. From there the asset can be staked through Jito, swapped on a Solana DEX, or simply held as a Solana-native token.

Why Solana Settlement Is Fast

Solana is a single high-throughput layer-one chain that targets sub-second block times rather than batching transactions through a rollup, a design covered on Wikipedia's Solana entry. Because there is no additional challenge window to wait through once the deposit lands, the Solana side of the transfer is typically the fastest leg of the whole route.

What You Can Bring to Solana

SOL itself, wrapped ETH, and major stablecoins such as USDC are the common deposits, and most routes also carry a wider set of tokens native to the source chain. Whatever arrives keeps its identity on Solana — bridged USDC still spends as USDC — and can be sent back the way it came whenever you want.

JitoSOL and Liquid Staking After a Jito Bridge

Once SOL is in a Solana wallet, the next step many people take is staking it through Jito rather than leaving it idle. Jito's liquid staking product issues JitoSOL, a token that represents staked SOL plus the validator rewards and MEV revenue Jito's infrastructure captures on top of ordinary staking yield, a mechanism documented in the Jito documentation. JitoSOL keeps earning while it sits in your wallet, and because it is a regular SPL token, it can still be traded, used as collateral or supplied to a liquidity pool without unstaking first. A deposit that lands as plain SOL can be converted to JitoSOL in a single additional transaction, so bridging and staking are two separate but easily chained steps rather than one locked-in action.

Jito Bridge Fees and Timing

The bridge charges whatever the source chain's network fee costs to run the deposit, plus any small protocol fee the route adds; once the asset lands on Solana, further activity is paid in SOL and costs a fraction of a cent. Timing is not symmetric across every route — deposits from a slower chain can take several minutes to finalize before Solana credits the balance, while moving funds back off Solana depends on the destination chain's own confirmation rules. Judge a Route by the Net Received Amount

Weigh any bridge quote by the amount that actually lands in the Solana wallet after every fee, not the headline rate advertised at the start. Small transfers can be dominated by a fixed source-chain gas cost, so a larger single deposit usually carries less overhead per dollar than several small ones. You can confirm what landed by looking up your Solana address on the Solana explorer.

Using a Jito Bridge, Step by Step

The sequence stays the same on almost every route, which is what makes the transfer predictable once you have done it once.
  • Pick the source chain and the asset you want to move, then set Solana as the destination.
  • Enter the amount; the route quotes the fee and the amount that will land in your Solana wallet.
  • Approve the token if the source chain requires it, then sign the deposit transaction.
  • Wait for the source chain to confirm — your Solana balance updates shortly after.
  • Optionally stake the SOL you received with Jito to receive JitoSOL, or move on to a Solana DEX.

Why the Solana Side Rarely Needs a Wait

Most of the delay in this kind of transfer sits on the source chain's own confirmation rules, not on Solana. Once the deposit is recognized, Solana's block time means the credited balance is usually visible before you have finished reading the confirmation message.

Is a Jito Bridge Safe to Use?

A canonical bridge route keeps your keys in your own wallet at every step, so the risks you actually manage are the ones present at the moment you sign: the destination address, the token contract, and the approval you grant on the source chain.

Approvals and Allowances on Solana

Solana's token program uses delegate approvals rather than the unlimited allowances common on EVM chains, but the same caution applies — grant a bridge contract only the amount it needs for the transfer in front of you, and review any standing delegate you no longer use.

The One Rule That Never Changes

A support agent, wallet prompt or bridge interface never has a legitimate reason to ask for your seed phrase. A genuine bridge interface only ever asks you to approve a transaction you can read in your own wallet before you sign it.

Bridging to Solana and the Jito DeFi Ecosystem

Reaching Solana is the on-ramp, not the destination. Once an asset lands on Solana, it can move straight into lending markets, decentralized exchanges and liquidity pools, many of which list their tracked value on DefiLlama's Solana page. JitoSOL in particular is accepted as collateral across several Solana money markets, letting staking rewards keep accruing while the token is also put to work elsewhere. Keep a small SOL balance for gas, since even a JitoSOL position still needs a native SOL fee to move.

Jito Bridge vs a Third-Party Bridge

Solana can be reached through a canonical lock-and-mint route or through a third-party liquidity bridge that fronts assets instead of waiting on finality. The canonical route mints the standard representation of the asset on Solana and is the steadier choice for a large or long-held position. A third-party route can complete faster because it advances liquidity rather than waiting for the source chain, but it adds its own fee and its own operator risk. Checking the method against the Solana documentation for the token standard in question is a reasonable way to confirm which kind of route a given bridge quote is actually using.

What does a Jito Bridge do?
It locks or burns your asset on the source chain and credits the matching amount on Solana, so the funds arrive ready to stake with Jito, trade, or sit in a wallet as JitoSOL.
What is JitoSOL?
JitoSOL is the liquid staking token Jito issues for staked SOL. It keeps earning validator and MEV rewards while remaining a regular SPL token you can trade or use as collateral.
What do I pay to bridge onto Solana?
You pay the source chain's network fee plus any small protocol fee to deposit. Once assets are on Solana, further activity is paid in SOL and costs a fraction of a cent per transaction.
How long does a Jito Bridge deposit take?
Most of the wait happens on the source chain while it confirms; Solana itself credits the balance within seconds of recognizing the deposit.
Do I need an account to bridge onto Solana?
No. You connect a self-custody wallet and bridge on-chain with no sign-up and no custody handed to a third party.
Can I move assets back off Solana?
Yes. The canonical route works both ways — the asset is burned on Solana and released on the source chain once its own confirmation rules are met.
Is SOL needed even if I only hold JitoSOL?
Yes. JitoSOL transactions, like any Solana transaction, still pay a small SOL network fee, so keep a small SOL balance on hand even if most of your position sits in JitoSOL.
